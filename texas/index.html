Nonprofit: forming in Texas

Texas Secretary of State. Certificate of Formation (Form 202) fee $25. No general charitable solicitation registration for most nonprofits. Apply for franchise tax exemption after IRS determination. Minimum 3 directors.

File Certificate of Formation: File Form 202 with Texas Secretary of State. Fee $25. Online via SOSDirect. Include 501(c)(3) purpose and dissolution language. Processing typically 3–5 business days online.

Federal steps (every state)

  1. Get an EIN, free online at IRS.gov.
  2. Adopt bylaws and open a dedicated bank account.
  3. Apply for 501(c)(3) status: Form 1023-EZ ($275) or Form 1023 ($600) via Pay.gov.
  4. Register at SAM.gov for a UEI, then set up Grants.gov (allow 1–4 weeks).
  5. Keep up with state registration, annual reports and the Form 990 series.

Small business funding in Texas

High SBA loan volume. Multiple district offices. Strong lender competition across the state.

SBA programs are national. Start with SBA Loans and SBA Lender Match; a local SBDC can review your documents for free.

Questions

Where do I file to start a nonprofit in Texas?

File Form 202 with Texas Secretary of State. Fee $25. Online via SOSDirect. Include 501(c)(3) purpose and dissolution language. Processing typically 3–5 business days online.

How much does it cost to apply for 501(c)(3) status?

IRS Form 1023-EZ costs $275 and the full Form 1023 costs $600, paid through Pay.gov. The EIN itself is free at IRS.gov.

Where can a small business in Texas get funding help?

High SBA loan volume. Multiple district offices. Strong lender competition across the state. SBA programs are national; SBA Lender Match (sba.gov/lendermatch) connects you with participating lenders.
